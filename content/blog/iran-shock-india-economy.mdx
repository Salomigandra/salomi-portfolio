## How a War Reaches Your Kitchen

When the United States launched strikes on Iranian nuclear facilities in April 2026, Brent crude crossed **$110/barrel** within 48 hours. The Strait of Hormuz — through which 20% of the world's seaborne oil passes — entered partial closure. India, which imports **88% of its crude oil**, had no insulation from what followed.

The rupee fell to **₹90.72/$** (May 2026 intraday lows). Foreign Portfolio Investors withdrew an estimated **$17–19 billion** in six weeks — the fastest FPI outflow since the 2013 taper tantrum. India's forex reserves dropped by approximately **$7.79 billion** in a month. The RBI intervened but could not stop the slide without exhausting reserves.

None of this was your decision. But all of it is affecting your budget.

## The Oil Import Arithmetic

India's **annual crude oil import bill** at $65/barrel was approximately $123 billion. At $110/barrel, the same volume costs roughly **$174.9 billion** — an additional $51.9 billion per year, or about **₹4.7 lakh crore**.

This is not an abstract government number. It flows through to:

- **Fuel prices**: Petrol and diesel are partially administered in India, but sustained high crude creates fiscal pressure to pass through costs. Every ₹10/litre increase in petrol costs a 2-wheeler owner (assuming 1,000 km/month at 45 km/L) approximately **₹222 extra per month**
- **LPG**: At $110 crude, an LPG cylinder's market price would be approximately **₹1,100–1,200**, vs. ₹912 before the conflict. HPCL, BPCL, and IOC (India's three major OMCs) collectively absorb over **₹800 crore/day** in under-recoveries
- **Food**: Fertiliser prices (linked to natural gas) rise with energy, pushing up production costs for wheat, rice, and vegetables
- **Rupee pass-through**: Every imported good — electronics, edibles, medicines — becomes more expensive when the rupee is at ₹90 vs. ₹83

## What PM Modi Asked India to Do — And Why the Math Works

PM Modi made four specific public asks in May 2026:

**1. Reduce fuel consumption (WFH 2 days/week, 2-wheeler carpooling)**
A typical 2-wheeler commuter covering 40 km/day, working 5 days/week, uses roughly 156 litres/month (at 45 km/L). Cutting 2 WFH days saves ~62 litres/month = **₹3,600–4,200/year** per household at current prices.

Nationally, if 20% of India's ~7 crore commuter 2-wheelers reduce consumption this way, the OMC relief is approximately **₹850–1,100 crore/month**.

**2. Reduce gold imports**
India imports 700–800 tonnes of gold per year — a significant current account drain. Every 10% reduction in gold demand saves approximately **$3–4 billion** annually in foreign exchange. This directly reduces rupee selling pressure.

**3. Reduce outbound foreign travel**
At ₹90/$, overseas travel costs 8.4% more than at ₹83/$. Each dollar spent abroad exits India's forex pool. A 10% reduction in leisure outbound travel (from India's ~2 crore annual outbound travellers) saves an estimated **$800M–1B** in annual forex.

**4. Shift to domestic alternatives**
Substituting imported goods with Indian-made equivalents reduces the trade deficit. For every ₹100 of imports substituted, approximately **₹7–10 of forex pressure** is relieved.
